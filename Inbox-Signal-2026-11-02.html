The five, if you only read five

ranked

Ranked picks for an engineering lead at a software consultancy: pieces that change how you would advise a client on AI-assisted delivery, review, governance and cost.

1
The AI Corner / TLDR DevRead (paywalled preview)

Claude Code's team now merges more than twice as many PRs, and reverts fell by over 40%

Anthropic's own account, relayed by TLDR and The AI Corner, says Claude writes about 90% of Claude Code's code and that merged PRs more than doubled from June to September while the share needing reverts fell by over 40%. The review design is layered: automated review before and after PR creation, human approval scaled by risk, and fast rollback. The AI Corner adds Faros AI data from 1,255 teams, where high AI adoption came with 98% more merged PRs, review time up 91% and bugs per developer up 9%. The Anthropic numbers are self-reported and 'reverted' is not defined.

2
The Pragmatic Engineer (The Pulse)Read

AI is tilting software security toward attackers, and a CA key failure and a PHP RCE pair show why

Orosz's week includes eMudhra, an intermediate certificate authority whose ECC private keys were tiny numbers from 30 April 2025, undetected for 18 months; its stock fell about 40% in two days. Two critical PHP RCE flaws were found by researchers using GPT-5.6 Sol, and exploitation began before disclosure. AWS saw an attempt four days before the CVEs went public. His prescriptions are automated patching, expecting day-zero exploits, and defenders using AI too.

3
The Pragmatic EngineerRead (paywalled)

AI SRE mostly speeds up investigation, not the autonomous fixes shown in demos

Agents have roughly doubled PR frequency, so incident rate rises, and Rootly data shows pages and out-of-hours pages nearly doubled. ResolveAI rebuilt its agent three times, and context rot and self-confirmation drove the rebuilds. Stripe's in-house agent takes 3 minutes for a simple run and 14 minutes for a deep one, and humans still approve every PR. The article is paywalled after about 60%, and the authors say vendors will likely beat in-house builds (Orosz is an investor in incident.io).

4
The Beautiful Mess (John Cutler)Read

The 'software factory' is partly identity defense, and it builds new silos

Cutler argues that when AI makes building cheap, engineers move up to design and govern the factory. That keeps their identity as builders and is also real adaptation. He asks whether a factory model fits end-to-end product work, since engineering, product and GTM factories each stop at their own profession's boundary and design is left in the middle. No figures; it is opinion, and a useful counter-narrative to the factory pitches in this week's mail.

5
The VC CornerRead

Usage and outcome pricing are replacing seats, and hybrid is now the most common model

Growth Unhinged's 2026 survey, quoted by The VC Corner, puts hybrid pricing at 37%, up from 25% a year earlier, and respondents think investors prefer hybrid 35% to 5% over seats. Zylo's data shows average SaaS spend per organisation up 8% to $55.7M while app count stayed about 305. The advice for startups is to name one countable unit, write down what 'resolved' means, and track unit cost from day one.

System Design NuggetsRead (paywalled)

Quorums work because any two majorities overlap; R + W > N gives read-after-write consistency

A majority of 3 nodes is 2, of 5 is 3, of 7 is 4. Four nodes tolerate one failure, the same as three, which is why odd sizes are recommended. With N=3, R=2/W=2 is balanced, R=1/W=3 gives fast reads but one failure blocks writes, and R=1/W=1 is only eventually consistent. The consensus section is paywalled.

System Design NuggetsRead (paywalled)

Latency, throughput and bandwidth are three different problems

Latency is time per operation, throughput is the achieved rate and bandwidth is the capacity ceiling. A New York to London round trip cannot beat about 55 ms because light in fibre is roughly 200,000 km/s, so only moving data closer lowers that floor. The article is paywalled after propagation and transmission delay.

System Design NuggetsRead

The 100-term system design glossary, with the claim that knowing these terms earns staff offers

The post defines 100 terms in four categories and argues that mechanism matters more than the label: for a circuit breaker, know the closed, open and half-open transitions. It gives 99.9% availability as about 8.7 hours of downtime a year. Its claim that candidates who know terms 76 to 100 receive staff offers is unsupported.

System Design NuggetsRead (paywalled)

Most teams should start with a modular monolith, but only enforced boundaries make it work

The post argues that a modular monolith without enforced boundaries is just a monolith with extra folders. A wrong module boundary costs about a day to fix, versus weeks for a wrong microservice boundary. The preview covers principles 1 to 5 of 20 and cuts off at principle 6.

System Design NuggetsRead (paywalled)

Neo Kim (systemdesignone)Read

Eleven scalability patterns for interviews, each with its trade-off

Sharding strategies compared: hash gives an even spread but is bad for range queries, range causes hot shards, and directory adds a dependency. Consistent hashing with virtual nodes limits data movement, while modulo hashing remaps keys when a shard is added. Its bottom line is to start small, measure, and scale when needed. Generic interview-prep content.

System Design NuggetsRead (paywalled)

Signed JWTs are misused: a signature proves integrity, not secrecy

A standard JWT is signed, not encrypted, so never put secrets in it. HS256 shares one key, so any verifying service can forge tokens; RS256 or ES256 keep the private key at the issuer. Rotate keys by publishing the new key and keeping the old public key until old tokens expire. The post is paywalled at Part 4.

Neo Kim (systemdesignone)Read (paywalled)

GitOps with ArgoCD: pull-based deploys shrink the blast radius of a compromised CI

Push-based CD puts cluster credentials in CI, so a compromised pipeline is effectively a compromised cluster. Pull-based ArgoCD runs inside the cluster, so a compromised CI can only change Git, which is reviewed and reconciled. The production-hardening sections are paywalled.
